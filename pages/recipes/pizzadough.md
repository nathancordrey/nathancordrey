# Caputo 00 Pizza Dough

A simple overnight pizza dough made with Caputo 00 flour. This recipe makes two 10- to 12-inch pizzas and works well in a home oven with a baking stone or steel.

## Ingredients

- 250 g Caputo 00 flour, about 2 cups
- 1/4 cup warm water
- 1/2 cup cool water
- 1/4 teaspoon active dry yeast
- 1 teaspoon fine sea salt
- 1/2 teaspoon granulated sugar
- Extra virgin olive oil, for coating the bowl

## Instructions

1. In a small bowl, stir the yeast and sugar into the warm water. Let it sit briefly while you prepare the mixer.
2. Add the cool water to the bowl of a stand mixer, then add the yeast mixture.
3. Using the paddle attachment on low speed, mix in about one-third of the flour. Increase speed slightly and mix for 1 to 2 minutes, until a smooth batter forms.
4. Add the remaining flour and the salt. Mix until the dough comes together.
5. Switch to the dough hook and knead on low to medium speed for 8 to 10 minutes.
6. Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a lightly oiled bowl. Cover with plastic wrap and let it rest at room temperature for about 1 1/2 hours, until slightly risen.
7. Divide the dough into two equal balls. Place them on a floured sheet pan, cover tightly with plastic wrap and a towel, and refrigerate for 24 hours.
8. Remove the dough from the refrigerator about 1 1/2 hours before making pizza so it can come to room temperature.
9. Preheat a baking stone or baking steel in the oven at the highest temperature your oven allows for about 1 hour.
10. Stretch each dough ball into a 10- to 12-inch pizza, add sauce and toppings, and bake until the crust is browned and the toppings are cooked.

## Notes

- The long refrigerated proof gives the crust better flavor and helps avoid an overly yeasty taste.
- If you need a same-day version, let the divided dough balls proof at room temperature for about 4 hours instead of refrigerating overnight. Watch carefully so they do not overproof.
- The sugar helps the crust brown better in a home oven.
- This dough uses very little yeast, so the first rise may be modest and does not need to double.
